## Frequently asked questions

## Difference Between Rheumatology And Orthopaedics Surgeon?

A Rheumatologist specializes in diagnosing and treating autoimmune diseases and musculoskeletal conditions, focusing on non-surgical management. On the other hand, an Orthopedic Surgeon is trained in surgical techniques to treat musculoskeletal issues like fractures, injuries, and joint replacements. While both deal with conditions affecting bones, joints, and muscles, Rheumatologists primarily use medications and therapies, while Orthopedic Surgeons perform surgical interventions.

## What Are Symptoms Of Rheumatoid Arthritis?

Symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis often include joint pain, swelling, stiffness, and tenderness, particularly in smaller joints such as those of the hands and feet. Other signs may include fatigue, fever, and weight loss. In advanced stages, RA can cause joint deformity and limited range of motion. The earlier the symptoms are diagnosed and treated, the better the chance of managing them and preventing joint damage. For advanced rheumatology treatment in Bangalore, consult Cytecare.

## Which Joints Are Affected In Rheumatoid Arthritis?

Rheumatoid arthritis primarily affects the small joints in your hands and feet. It can also affect other joints, such as your wrists, elbows, shoulders, knees, and ankles.

## Cause Of Rheumatoid Arthritis?

Rheumatoid arthritis is primarily caused by an overactive immune system mistakenly attacking healthy joint tissues. This autoimmune response triggers inflammation, leading to pain, stiffness, and joint damage characteristic of RA. While the exact cause remains unknown, genetic predisposition, environmental factors, and hormonal changes may contribute to its development.

## Is every joint pain a sign of arthritis?

Not necessarily! Joint pain can result from injury, overuse, infections, or other conditions. But if it's persistent, especially with swelling or stiffness, it could be a sign of something more—like rheumatoid arthritis or osteoarthritis. It's worth checking with a rheumatologist if it doesn't go away.
