## Common Signs and Symptoms of Gynaecological Problems

- Bleeding between periods.
- Abnormal vaginal bleeding.
- Frequent and urgent need to urinate, or a burning sensation during
  urination.

- Bleeding after menopause.
- Pain or pressure in your pelvis that differs from menstrual cramps.
- Itching, burning, swelling, redness, or soreness in the vaginal area.

- Increased vaginal discharge.
- Vaginal discharge with an unpleasant or unusual odor, or of a unusual color.

## Frequently asked questions

## Can Gynaecologists Treat Male Infertility?

Gynaecologists can't treat male infertility. Gynaecologists specialize in the female reproductive system, focusing on issues related to women's health, pregnancy, and childbirth. Male infertility is typically addressed by urologists or reproductive endocrinologists who specialize in male reproductive health and fertility problems.

## What is The Difference Between Gynaecologists and Obstetricians?

Gynaecologists specialize in women's reproductive health, focusing on issues like menstrual disorders, contraception, and fertility treatments. Obstetricians, on the other hand, are experts in pregnancy, childbirth, and postpartum care. While both professions overlap in some areas, gynaecologists primarily deal with non-pregnancy-related concerns, while obstetricians focus on prenatal care and delivery.

## When Should You See a Gynaecologist?

You should consult a gynaecologist when you face issues like abnormal periods, pain during intercourse, pelvic pain, difficulties with pregnancy, and menopause-related symptoms.

## What is a PAP Test?

A PAP test, also known as a Pap smear, is a screening procedure for cervical cancer. It involves collecting cells from the cervix to check for abnormalities that could indicate the presence of cancer or precancerous conditions. Women are advised to have regular Pap tests as part of their preventive healthcare routine, as early detection can lead to effective treatment and improved outcomes.

## What Does a UTI Feel Like For a Woman?

Common sensations include a persistent urge to urinate, accompanied by a burning sensation or pain during urination. You may notice cloudy or strong-smelling urine, and in some cases, even traces of blood. Additionally, lower abdominal discomfort or pressure are often reported.
